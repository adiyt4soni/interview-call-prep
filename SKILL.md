# Interview and call prep

The pattern: a short "read this first" list of risks, answers written in the candidate's
own voice from true facts only, and a cue card small enough to glance at during the call.

## 0. Rules
- **Facts about the candidate come only from their profile file** (CV source, canonical profile, dated corrections).
  Never invent a metric, tool, title or result. If a likely question has no true strong answer, say so and write the
  honest version (the gap, then the real mitigation).
- **Respect the profile's "never mention" list** (anything the user has ruled out).
  List those items in the pack's Don'ts section so they are in front of the user.
- **Facts about the company are sourced and dated** (their site, filings, press, the job ad). Coaching advice from
  public interview guides is labelled as **coaching judgement, not the company's rubric**.
- Tag key claims **certain / likely / guessing**.
- Read the job ad and every email in the thread before writing. Check the calendar entry for time, format, link and
  interviewer. If something conflicts (two times, two names), put it at the top as OPEN.
- Never accept or move a time, and never email the interviewer. Draft only.

## 1. Gather (in this order)
1. The calendar invite and the email thread: date, time and time zone, format (phone, video, in person, assessment
   platform), duration, who (name and role from the invite or email only), anything they asked the user to prepare.
2. The job ad (saved copy and the live page; note if the live page has gone).
3. The application as sent: which CV and cover letter version, and the form answers. The interviewer will have read
   these, so the answers must match them.
4. The company: what they sell and to whom, size, stage, recent news (last 90 days), customers named on their own
   site, the team the role sits in. Keep a source list with the date each page was read.
5. What this kind of round is for (recruiter screen = motivation, basics, logistics, salary; hiring manager = can
   you do the job; founder = do they trust you; assessment = the format and timing rules; final = fit and closing).
   Use two or more public guides and label it coaching judgement.

## 2. Write the pack (`<company>-<round>-prep-YYYY-MM-DD.md`)
1. **READ THIS FIRST: three things that could sink the call.** The most likely failure points for this candidate in
   this round (a requirement they don't meet, a question with a weak answer, a logistics trap). One line each, with
   what to do about it.
2. **Logistics.** Time in the user's time zone, format, link or address, who, duration, what to have open.
3. **What this round is for.** Two or three sentences, labelled coaching judgement.
4. **The company in 60 seconds.** Five to eight sourced lines the user can say out loud.
5. **The answers, in the user's voice.** The 10-13 most likely questions for this round, each with a short spoken
   answer (30-60 seconds): tell me about yourself; why this company; why this role; the gap question the ad
   implies; a process or method question; a win; a failure; how you use AI or tools; why you're leaving or what
   you're doing now; what you know about their customers; logistics (start date, salary expectation from the
   user's own number, location, standard answers from the profile).
6. **If they ask you to do it live** (sell me something, open a call, walk through a case): a short worked example.
7. **Questions to ask them.** Four or five specific ones; the user picks two.
8. **Don'ts.** The never-mention list and any claim that would not survive a follow-up question.
9. **Tonight: 20 minutes.** A checklist: say the 60-second story out loud twice, read the three risks, test the
   link, have the CV open.
10. **Sources** with dates checked.

## 3. Write the cue card (`<company>-<round>-cue-card.md`)
One screen: the 60-second story as bullets, three proof points, the "why them" in one line, the two questions to
ask, the three don'ts, and the logistics line. Nothing the user has to read in full during the call.

## 4. Check before handing over
- Every company fact has a source and a date. Every candidate fact traces to the profile.
- Count the never-mention items in the pack outside the Don'ts section: it must be 0.
- The answers match the CV and form answers that were actually sent.
- Read the 60-second story aloud at a normal pace and time it: 50-75 seconds.
- State what could not be found (for example "interviewer's role not public") as an open item, not a guess.

## 5. After the call
Ask the user for notes while fresh: questions asked, what landed, what didn't, next step and date. Append a dated
entry to the project's memory file, add the next step to the tracker, and, if there is another round, start that
pack from these notes.
